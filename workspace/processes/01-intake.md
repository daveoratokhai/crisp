Intake is the first stage of Strategy/Discovery. Its job is to gather what we need to understand a client's business well enough to identify where they have problems, gaps, or leakage of value, without yet applying our own outside-in analysis to it.

Historically this was a static form: a fixed list of questions, filled in independently by each respondent. We are replacing that with an intake chatbot — a conversational interview agent that talks to each respondent individually, adapting its questions to what they've already said rather than marching through a fixed script.

The interview runs in two passes. Pass 1 is a wide net: every respondent is interviewed independently, so we get each person's view of the business and its workflows without it being shaped by anyone else's answers. Pass 2 is a deep dive, run only with the person or people who actually live a given workflow day to day, to get the detail a first-pass answer can't carry.

What comes out of intake is not a finished picture. It's raw, per-respondent material — workflow mentions, described pain points, named systems and tools — each one tied back to who said it and how directly they experience it (lived it themselves vs. heard about it secondhand vs. assumed). That distinction matters downstream: it's what lets later stages say a figure was measured, estimated, or guessed, rather than presenting everything with false confidence.

Intake output feeds directly into assessment. It is also the first version of the material that eventually becomes the client's workflow map — the map that stays in use for the rest of the engagement.
