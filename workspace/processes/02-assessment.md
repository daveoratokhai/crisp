Assessment takes the raw material gathered in intake and applies Evercrisp's own outside-in analysis to it. Where intake captures what the client's people say about their own business, assessment is where we bring our own point of view: research on the company, its market and competitors, and where AI is already reshaping that industry, read against the Value Flow Engine framework (Need, Visibility, Value Flow, Delivered Value).

Individual intake mentions also start getting clustered here — related mentions from different respondents about the same underlying workflow or problem get grouped into a single, canonical picture of that workflow, so the same issue named by several people in different words is recognized as one thing, not several. Every claim keeps its provenance: how many respondents named it, and whether the numbers behind it are measured, estimated, or guessed.

This clustered, outside-in view is what gets presented back to executives: a 90-120 minute discussion where we walk through what we found, validate it against what leadership actually knows, and surface where the real gaps are. It's not us telling the client something they haven't seen at all — it's us naming, organizing, and sharpening what's often already half-visible to them, so it becomes something they can act on.

Assessment output sets up the workshop: it tells us where to focus, and gives leadership their own reason to bring the broader team into the process.
