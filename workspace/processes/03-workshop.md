The workshop widens the lens from the executive-level assessment to the organization as a whole. Where assessment worked from a smaller set of respondents and leadership's own reaction to it, the workshop pulls in a broader cross-section of the team — the people who actually run the day-to-day workflows the business depends on.

This is where the client's own people surface and name the specific problems and workflows they want addressed, in their own words. For Givebacks, this produced 59 named workflows spanning the organization. Some of these get flagged by several people independently — a sign the issue is real and shared, not one person's pet complaint — and some turn out to only exist because one specific person happens to carry that knowledge or task alone, a risk in itself.

The workshop is run as a facilitated session, typically a day to a day and a half, in person when it can be. It builds directly on the clustering and outside-in view already established in assessment, but goes further: it isn't just us presenting a point of view, it's the client's people actively contributing and prioritizing alongside us.

What comes out of the workshop is a raw, organization-wide list of everything worth addressing — the direct input to the opportunity backlog.
