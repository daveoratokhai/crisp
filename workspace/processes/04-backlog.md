The opportunity backlog is where everything surfaced in the workshop gets organized into something actionable. Every workflow or problem raised is grouped by theme and prioritized, rather than left as a flat, undifferentiated list.

Not everything on the list warrants the same kind of attention. Some items are large, cross-cutting opportunities that genuinely need executive sponsorship and Evercrisp's direct involvement to build. Others are smaller, more contained problems that the client's own team can learn to solve themselves once they have the right skills and a bit of AI literacy — these become candidates for training rather than for a build engagement.

This is also the point where the client's own language and priorities start to matter more than our own generic vocabulary: a client will often reorganize or rename what we handed them into their own framing (their own initiative names, their own sequencing), and that's expected and fine — the underlying backlog and its themes stay traceable back to what the workshop actually surfaced.

The opportunity backlog is the bridge between discovery, which happens once per client, and implementation, which repeats — one opportunity at a time, in priority order, out of this backlog. It also lives on past that first pass: as new workflows surface or priorities shift over the course of the engagement, the backlog gets revisited rather than treated as a one-time snapshot.
